The Terminal Cathedral · Privacy details

What gets recorded?

This website has a small, optional statistics script. Dan built it himself, because he is curious about how the site is used. It only runs if you choose YES · ALLOW STATS in the Registrar's observation request. If you choose NO · THANKS, or make no choice, nothing is counted or sent, and the website works exactly the same. Without a YES given on the homepage, nothing is counted anywhere on the site.

Who runs it, and why

The site and its statistics script are made and run by Dan Almer Jensen, as a personal project. The statistics are just for his own curiosity. They are not used for advertising or marketing. The site does not use Google Analytics, advertising trackers or any other analytics product.

What is counted, if you choose YES

The script counts things, it does not keep a record of you. Each time one of these happens, the site sends one tiny message that names only what happened, for example "Music opened":

  • the homepage or the original Registrar console is viewed, and whether it stayed open for at least 10 seconds;
  • a section is opened (Electronics, AI, Software, Music, The Lab, The Human), or a record inside one is expanded;
  • one of the site's links is followed (for example AI-Madpakken on GitHub, Album Zero or Album One), or the original Registrar console is entered;
  • in the original console: the terminal is entered, one of its links is followed, a part of the album passage is reached, or a choice leads to its "Routing error";
  • a script error happens on the page (only that it happened, not the error text);
  • when you leave a page: roughly how long it was visible on screen after you chose YES, as one of six ranges (under 10 seconds, 10–30 seconds, 30–60 seconds, 1–3 minutes, 3–10 minutes, over 10 minutes).

The message contains nothing else: no visit or session number, no page address, no address of the page you came from, no time, no device or browser details, and nothing you typed. The site does not create any identifier for you, and nothing connects one message to another, or the homepage to the original console.

Where the counts are kept

The messages go to a small Google Apps Script that Dan wrote, running on Google's servers (script.google.com). It adds one to a daily total in a spreadsheet, with three columns: date, what happened, and how many times that day. It does not save the messages themselves or a list of visits. Google's Apps Script does keep its own technical log of when the script ran (time, duration, success or failure), visible only in Dan's account; it holds none of the message content. The date is set by the script, not by your browser. The spreadsheet is in Dan's own Google Drive, and only Dan has access to it.

Google provides the service the script and the spreadsheet run on. To deliver each message, Google's servers necessarily receive your IP address, your browser's standard request details and the website's address. The messages are sent without cookies, so a Google account you are signed in to is not attached to them. Dan's script does not receive or store these, but Google handles them as described in Google's own privacy policy.

Are the counts anonymous?

The spreadsheet only holds daily totals, with no identifiers, so it does not show who did what. One honest limitation: this is a small website, so on a quiet day a total can be just 1. Someone who already knows you visited that day could then guess that the 1 was you.

What is stored on your device

Only your choice: one entry in your browser's local storage for this website, named tc_observation, with the value granted or denied. It contains no identifier and no date. It lets both parts of the site follow your choice and keeps the question from appearing again. No cookies are used. If your browser does not allow local storage, nothing is stored: statistics stay off, a YES applies only to the page you are on, and the question is asked again next time.

Changing your mind

Use OBSERVATION PREFERENCES at the bottom of the homepage to switch statistics on or off at any time. Switching off stops all further messages immediately, on the homepage and in the original Registrar console. Clearing this website's data in your browser also removes your choice, and the question will be asked again.
